## Label
Cytoplasm, packed tight with molecules

## Paragraph
This is the cytoplasm: everything inside your cell outside the nucleus, a watery gel crowded with organelles and molecules. Up close it is packed. Every cubic micrometer, a box a thousandth of a millimeter on each side, holds 2 to 4 million proteins [inv-cell]. Molecules jostle shoulder to shoulder, with little open space between them [inv-crowd].

## Go deeper
Your cell holds about 10 billion (10¹⁰) protein molecules, of perhaps 10,000 kinds [inv-cell]. In a liver cell, the gel between the organelles, called the cytosol, fills 54% of the cell's volume [inv-vol]. By weight, a cell is 70% water and 18% protein [inv-cell].

What you see here is a stand-in. It shows that the space is crowded, but it leaves out the water and draws far fewer molecules than a real cell holds.
