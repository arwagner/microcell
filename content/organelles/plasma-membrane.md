## Label
The thin skin around your whole cell

## Paragraph
This is the plasma membrane, the skin that wraps your whole cell. It is a double layer of fatty molecules called lipids, only about 5 nanometers (nm, billionths of a meter) thick [used]. Proteins float in it, and some act as gates that control what gets in and what stays out [pm].

## Go deeper
Your cell keeps very different salt levels on the two sides of this skin. Inside there is about 140 millimolar (mM, a measure of how concentrated something is) potassium and 5 to 15 mM sodium; outside, about 5 mM potassium and 145 mM sodium [pm].

It is only a small part of your cell's membrane. In a liver cell, the plasma membrane is just 2% of all the membrane; the rest lines the organelles inside [pm].
