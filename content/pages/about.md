You are exploring one human cell. Here is where its numbers come from, and what we changed so you can see inside it.

## The reference cell
There is no single "typical" human cell. Cells in your body range from white blood cells about 7 µm (micrometers, thousandths of a millimeter) across to liver cells of 50 to 100 µm [inv-read].

So this app follows one reference cell: HeLa, a human cell line grown in labs, wherever HeLa data exists. Where it doesn't, gaps are filled from other mammalian cells, mostly liver cells and COS-7 monkey cells, and each borrowed figure says which cell it came from [inv-read].

## What is simplified
- **No water or ions.** Water makes up 70% of a real cell's weight [inv-cell]. Ions, which are atoms carrying an electric charge, such as potassium and sodium, are everywhere too [inv-ions]. We leave both out so you can see the machinery.
- **Crowding is not to scale.** A real cell packs 2 to 4 million proteins into every cubic micrometer [inv-cell]. We draw far fewer, so you can see past them.
- **Time is slowed.** A ribosome adds about 6 building blocks (amino acids) to a protein every second [proc-protein]. ATP synthase spins tens to a few hundred turns a second [proc-atp]. We slow each process down so you can follow it, and show how much it was slowed.
