You are exploring one human cell. Here is where its numbers come from, and what we changed so you can see inside it.

## The reference cell
There is no single "typical" human cell. Cells in your body range from white blood cells about 7 µm (micrometers, thousandths of a millimeter) across to liver cells of 50 to 100 µm [inv-read].

So this app follows one reference cell: HeLa, a human cell line grown in labs, wherever HeLa data exists. Where it doesn't, gaps are filled from other mammalian cells (mammals are the animal group that includes us), mostly liver cells and COS-7, a monkey cell line [inv-read]. Borrowed figures say which cell they came from.

## What is simplified
- **No water or ions.** Water makes up 70% of a typical cell's weight [inv-cell]. Ions, which are atoms carrying an electric charge, such as potassium and sodium, are everywhere too [inv-ions]. We leave both out so you can see the machinery.
- **Crowding is not to scale.** A real cell packs 2 to 4 million proteins into every cubic micrometer [inv-cell]. We draw far fewer, so you can see past them.
- **Time is slowed.** A ribosome, the machine that builds proteins, adds about 6 building blocks (amino acids) a second, measured in mouse stem cells [proc-protein]. ATP synthase, a motor that makes the fuel molecule ATP, spins tens to hundreds of turns a second, judged mostly from bacteria [proc-atp]. We slow each down and show by how much.
