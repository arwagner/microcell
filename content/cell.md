## Label
One human cell, the place you explore

## Paragraph
You are inside one human cell, about 15 micrometers (µm, thousandths of a millimeter) across, typical for an animal cell [inv-cell]. A thin skin, the plasma membrane, holds in a crowded, watery gel full of organelles, small structures that each do a job. Its numbers come from HeLa, a lab-grown human cell line, with gaps filled from other mammalian cells.

## Go deeper
There is no single "typical" human cell. Cells in your body range from white blood cells about 7 µm across to liver cells of 50 to 100 µm [inv-read]. So this app follows one reference cell.

The reference cell is HeLa, a human cell line grown in labs, wherever HeLa data exists. Where it doesn't, gaps are filled from other mammalian cells, mostly liver cells and COS-7 monkey cells, and each borrowed figure says which cell it came from [inv-read].

A HeLa cell holds 1,000 to 10,000 cubic micrometers [inv-cell]. That is enough room for its nucleus, its organelles and about 10 billion protein molecules [inv-cell].
