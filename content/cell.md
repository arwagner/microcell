## Label
One human cell, the place you explore

## Paragraph
You are inside one human cell, about 15 micrometers (µm, thousandths of a millimeter) across, typical for an animal cell [inv-cell]. A thin skin, the plasma membrane, holds in a crowded, watery gel full of organelles, small structures that each do a job. Its numbers come from HeLa, a lab-grown human cell line, with gaps filled from other mammalian cells.

## Go deeper
There is no single "typical" human cell. Cells in your body range from white blood cells about 7 µm across to liver cells of 50 to 100 µm [inv-read]. So this app follows one reference cell.

The reference cell is HeLa, a human cell line grown in labs, wherever HeLa data exists. Where it doesn't, gaps are filled from other mammalian cells (mammals are the animal group that includes us), mostly liver cells and COS-7, a monkey cell line [inv-read]. Borrowed figures say which cell they came from.

A HeLa cell holds 1,000 to 10,000 cubic micrometers [inv-cell]. A typical cell holds about 10 billion protein molecules, the workers that do most of its jobs [inv-cell]. The 15 µm width above is a textbook figure for a typical animal cell, not a HeLa measurement [inv-cell].
